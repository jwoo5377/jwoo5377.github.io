## Starting with everyday use

Residents continually adapt their homes. Balconies become gardens or storage, and overlooked corners gain new purposes. This project takes those everyday modifications as a starting point for imagining how existing apartments could accommodate change.

The proposal uses inflatable units to introduce an adaptable layer of space within and around an existing building. Rather than presenting a completed construction system, the project explores a spatial possibility through drawings, modular studies, and rendered views.

## The proposal

The design process moved from observations of residents’ spatial adaptations to a family of inflatable units. Studies explored unit combinations and their relationship to existing walls, slabs, circulation, and supporting structures.

Plans, sections, and visualizations show how these units could change the experience and use of an existing apartment framework.

## My contribution

This was a two-person project with **Donghyun Kim**, who led the overall concept. I participated in ideation and contributed primarily to **rendering and visualization**.

My contribution focused on communicating the proposal’s spatial relationships, material qualities, and overall atmosphere. The work reflects my background in spatial design and my interest in how people shape the environments they use.

## Project scope

Balloon Apartment is a speculative studio proposal. The visualizations communicate the design concept and intended spatial experience; construction feasibility, structural performance, and environmental impact remain subjects for further study.
